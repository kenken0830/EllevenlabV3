# Spoken Japanese rules for this repo

## Core target
Turn writing into speech that sounds like one thoughtful person talking to one listener.
The listener should feel "this person gets it" before they feel "this person is explaining it."

## Priority order
1. empathy first
2. natural spoken rhythm
3. light forward motion
4. explanation only after temperature is set

## Opening rules
- The first line should mirror the listener's situation or feeling.
- Start close to the listener, not with a definition, summary, or general statement.
- Prefer openings like:
  - "これ、ありますよね"
  - "こういう日、ありますよね"
  - "なんか止まる日ってありますよね"
- Avoid openings like:
  - "多くの人が..."
  - "〜とは..."
  - "最近〜が注目されています"
  - topic definitions such as "朝活って..."

## Convert writing into speech
- Prefer speech that sounds natural aloud.
- Put empathy before explanation.
- Use shorter thought units.
- Remove stacked explanations.
- Reduce "correct but stiff" phrasing.
- Let some lines feel slightly unfinished or soft, instead of making every sentence land hard.
- Prefer language a warm friend or trusted creator would actually say out loud.

## Good spoken qualities
- sounds like one person talking to one person
- feels human before it feels organized
- moves in small turns instead of lecture blocks
- leaves a little air in the line without overusing pauses
- makes the next sentence feel like a continuation, not a new slide

## Body rules
- Keep the opening empathy rules intact, then let the body stay close to the listener instead of turning into an explanation block.
- The body should sound like "what is probably happening here" or "this is why it feels stuck" before it sounds like advice.
- Let the body move in small spoken turns, where each line feels like a continuation of the previous line.
- A small next step is allowed, but it should arrive naturally inside the flow, not as a formal recommendation section.
- In the body, stay mostly in recognition, reframe, and gentle observation. Save the clearest action push for the close.
- Prefer concrete, lightly spoken lines over abstract summaries.
- If the body sounds like seminar notes, article summary, or self-help bullet points, rewrite it.
- If the body starts to feel neatly organized, loosen it until it sounds sayable in one breath.
- Avoid body lines that announce structure or teaching intent, such as:
  - "まずは"
  - "次に"
  - "ポイントは"
  - "ここで大事なのは"
  - "大切なのは"
  - "具体的には"
  - "要するに"
  - "つまり"
- Avoid body lines that summarize the lesson from above instead of staying in the listener's moment.
- Prefer a body that feels like a person walking alongside the listener, not presenting a mini talk.

## What to aim for
- "I get how that feels"
- "let me say this simply"
- "this is probably what is happening"
- "here is the small next step"
- "you do not have to fix everything right now"

## Sentence-shape rules
- Keep most lines to one idea.
- Limit explanation chains such as cause + contrast + conclusion in one line.
- Use connectors sparingly.
- Vary endings so every line does not close with the same weight.
- It is okay to use soft spoken endings when natural, such as:
  - "〜なんですよね"
  - "〜でいいと思います"
  - "〜かもしれません"
  - "〜くらいで十分です"

## What to avoid
- textbook phrasing
- speech that sounds like slides being read aloud
- too many perfectly closed sentence endings
- too much rhetorical structure like "first / next / therefore"
- body sections that sound like seminar summaries or article recap
- lecture connectors such as:
  - "まず"
  - "具体的には"
  - "次のステップとして"
  - "重要です"
  - "大切です"
  - "お伝えします"
- summary lines that sound like conclusions before the listener is warmed up

## Rewrite heuristics
- If a line sounds correct but stiff, make it simpler.
- If a line explains the topic, rewrite it to reflect the listener's felt experience.
- If three lines in a row are equally strong and closed, soften one of them.
- If the script sounds polished, make it more speakable.
